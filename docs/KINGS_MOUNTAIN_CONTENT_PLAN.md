### Draft 2 — Automatic transfer switches

**Suggested title:** What does an automatic transfer switch do?

**Post copy:**

An automatic transfer switch is the part of a standby system that detects a utility interruption and safely changes the home's selected electrical source. It is not a shortcut around electrical design. The switch, generator, fuel supply, panel, loads, and site conditions must work as one system. Required final design, permits, proposals, and installation belong with appropriately licensed professionals. Our Kings Mountain guide explains the planning conversation in plain English.

### Draft 3 — Understanding generator kW

**Suggested title:** Generator size is about loads, not just square footage

**Post copy:**

A generator's kW rating describes power capacity, but a useful plan starts with the actual equipment a household wants to support. Motors in well pumps, refrigerators, and HVAC equipment can draw extra current when starting. Fuel choice, load priority, panel configuration, and future needs also matter. A preliminary conversation can organize the questions; a licensed professional must complete the final electrical design and installation scope.

### Draft 7 — Professional load assessment

**Suggested title:** Why a real load assessment matters

**Post copy:**

Two similar homes can have very different backup priorities. One may depend on a well pump and home office; another may prioritize refrigeration and selected heating or cooling. A useful assessment identifies the loads, their starting requirements, and what can be managed during an outage. That information supports—not replaces—the licensed professional's final design, proposal, permit work, and installation plan.
